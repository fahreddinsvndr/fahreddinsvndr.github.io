Push pull legs (PPL) workout plan — and an app to log it

Push pull legs is one of the simplest ways to organise strength training: three workout days, each with a clear job. Below is a 6-day PPL routine you can start this week, how to progress on it, and how Liftset turns it into a log you can fill in seconds between sets.

The 6-day push pull legs routine

Run push, pull, legs, then repeat, and rest on the seventh day. Three days a week (each day once) works too if you are starting out.

DayExercises (sets × reps)
PushBench press 4×5–8 · Overhead press 3×8–10 · Incline dumbbell press 3×8–12 · Lateral raise 3×12–15 · Triceps pushdown 3×10–12
PullDeadlift or barbell row 3×5–8 · Pull-up or lat pulldown 3×8–10 · Seated cable row 3×10–12 · Face pull 3×12–15 · Biceps curl 3×10–12
LegsSquat 4×5–8 · Romanian deadlift 3×8–10 · Leg press 3×10–12 · Leg curl 3×10–12 · Calf raise 4×12–15

How to progress

  1. Work inside a rep range. Pick a weight you can lift for the bottom of the range with good form.
  2. Add reps before weight. When every set reaches the top of the range, add the smallest step next time: about 2.5 kg / 5 lb on squats and presses, less on dumbbells.
  3. Rest long enough. 2–3 minutes on the big lifts, 60–90 seconds on accessories.
  4. Deload a stalled lift. If a lift does not move for three sessions, drop it about 10% and build back up.
  5. Write everything down. Progressive overload only works when you know what you lifted last time.

Frequently asked questions

Is push pull legs good for beginners?

Yes. Start with three days a week (push, pull and legs once each) and lighter weights, then move to six days once recovery feels easy.

Can I do PPL at home?

Yes, with dumbbells and a pull-up bar: swap barbell presses for dumbbell presses, rows for one-arm rows and squats for goblet or split squats.

Do I need an app?

Not strictly — a notebook works. An app saves time between sets, remembers last session's numbers and shows whether you are actually getting stronger.
